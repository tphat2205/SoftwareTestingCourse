# AI Critique

In this assignment, the AI demonstrated significant limitations when applying strict ISTQB testing techniques, frequently producing incomplete or invalid artifacts. Primarily, the AI struggled with the principle of fault isolation; when designing test cases for Invalid Equivalence Classes, it often combined multiple invalid inputs into a single test case, risking fault masking. Furthermore, it fundamentally misunderstood when to apply Boundary Value Analysis (BVA), erroneously applying it to nominal identifiers like "Order ID" rather than restricting it to ordinal business variables. It also failed to consistently apply the 3-value BVA technique, missing LB+1 edge cases, and generated non-deterministic expected outcomes.

These failures occurred because the AI operates on probabilistic pattern matching rather than deterministic rule engines. Without explicit constraints, the AI cannot inherently distinguish between a technical database artifact and a true business boundary. It also tends to optimize for concise output, leading it to skip tedious but necessary steps like establishing precise preconditions unless heavily prompted.

The core principle learned from this collaboration is that AI should be treated as a rapid structural drafter rather than an autonomous domain expert. It excels at generating boilerplate Markdown structures and extracting variables, but it absolutely requires a human engineer to enforce strict ISTQB rules, validate boundary logic, and ensure deterministic outcomes. Effective AI collaboration in QA demands rigorous prompt engineering and uncompromising manual review to prevent systemic testing blind spots.
